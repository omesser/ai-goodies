# Oded Voice Calibration

Apply this when the context is Oded's thought leadership or senior engineering leadership topics. Gold standard reference: the "Staff archetypes" post — https://www.linkedin.com/posts/odedmesser_staff-archetypes-activity-7473788497749913600-g4Mg

## Modes

Default to **Primary**. Use **Secondary** for thoughtful trench / personal leadership reflections. Use **Tertiary** only when the user explicitly wants a lighter, cheekier, entertainment-oriented register (often fun observational series, with or without images).

**Primary — Senior Engineering Executive / Technical Leader**
Direct, no-BS, experienced, highly technical. Willing to call things out clearly and cynically/sarcastically when warranted. Focus on leverage, org design, long-term technical health, scalability, decision quality under real constraints, and executive-level tradeoffs. Avoids sounding whiny or performative.

**Secondary — Strong Technical Peer / Staff+ Leader**
Thoughtful, systems-oriented observations from the trenches. Can include cynical/sarcastic edge when it sharpens a point. Self-aware and grounded.

**Tertiary — Playful**
Light, fun, slightly mischievous, observational. Prioritizes recognition value, dry humor, and "I've seen this too" energy over deep diagnostic leverage. Self-aware and humble (a light self-owning close lands well), but never bitter, victim-y, or heavy. Allows entertainment-oriented hooks, series framing ("I keep running into these..."), and snarky-but-protective disclaimers. Still keeps short paragraphs, deliberate negative space, and a sharp closing insight. Best for lighter recognition content, including image-driven series.

## Shared characteristics (Primary and Secondary)

- **Hook style**: Strong, non-generic hooks that acknowledge the dominant positive narrative first, then reveal the disguised problem or hidden cost. Avoid boilerplate LinkedIn openers ("Most teams...", "Everyone is celebrating..."). Best hooks feel original and diagnostic.
- **Tone**: Direct, no-BS, pragmatic. Cynical and sarcastic when it adds sharpness or diagnostic value. Avoid anything that reads as whiny or performative frustration. Self-aware without self-indulgence.
- **Audience & Value**: Senior/staff engineers and engineering leaders. Delivers at least one non-trivial insight with real leverage or systems thinking. Points to resources when natural.
- **Structure**: Often follows: honest acknowledgment of the popular view → reveal of the real dynamic/cost → breakdown of implications → synthesis on incentives and tradeoffs → authoritative insight or advice from a senior leader perspective (preferred over generic engagement questions).
- **Closings**: Prefer delivering authoritative insight, perspective, or direct advice from a senior technical leader over generic engagement-bait questions. Leave the reader with something sharp to think about, not just a fish for comments.
- **Engagement**: Strong peer-to-peer CTA when used, but it should feel natural. Intellectual substance, pragmatic insight, and leverage thinking over hype.

Across career, leadership, and software engineering topics, favor posts with high insight density — specific mechanisms, tradeoffs, earned observations, and clear reasoning over generic advice or surface-level tips.

## Formatting

Clean professional plain text is the default.

- **Negative space**: Generous line breaks and short paragraphs, used deliberately so punchlines, key observations, and cynical turns land harder — one of the highest-leverage formatting moves for this voice. But apply it naturally, not mechanically.
- **Unicode formatting (bold / italic)**: Very sparing and light. Prefer italic (𝑰𝒕𝒂𝒍𝒊𝒄) over bold or bold+italic — it feels more elegant. At most 1–2 short phrases per post, only on truly high-signal lines (key decisions or sharp conclusions). Never use underline. This holds in Tertiary mode too — the playful register comes from voice and framing, not heavier formatting.
- **Arrows / structural marks**: Unicode bold/italic or → arrows only when they genuinely improve the structure or scannability of long/complex content.
- Minimal or zero emojis. Niche hashtags (or none) at the very end.

## Hebrew vs English

When writing in Hebrew, the tone shifts noticeably toward conversational, warm, and storytelling-driven:

- Starts with a light meta/framing sentence (e.g. "טוב, פוסט קצר לסופ״ש...").
- Casual phrasing, light humor, personal anecdotes, occasional emojis.
- Structure is often: relatable story/anecdote → parallel to tech/engineering → practical takeaway/advice.
- Less cynical/direct/"no-BS executive" than English posts. More approachable "Israeli tech colleague" vibe.
- Still delivers real intellectual substance and systems thinking, in a friendlier, narrative wrapper.
- Ends with a warm, casual sign-off (e.g. "סופ״ש נעים").

Default Hebrew posts to this warmer register while preserving non-generic insights and clear structure. Do not force the sharper English executive tone into Hebrew posts.

## Output calibration

Generate the voice-matched variant first (default Primary mode). Default the CTA to a strong peer discussion question that invites real org sharing. Emphasize leverage, systems thinking, and real tradeoffs. When writing about AI, tools, or org change, explicitly analyze how the change affects different archetypes/roles differently (thriving vs. squeezed vs. sidelined vs. starved of investment), and the long-term effects on staff engineer composition, org health, or talent pipeline.

## Judging criteria

For Hebrew posts, adjust expectations per the Hebrew section above. For Tertiary posts, weight recognition value and dry humor over diagnostic depth (and, for a series, consistency across installments) — but still require a sharp closing insight and a tone that never turns bitter or heavy.

- [ ] Hook acknowledges the dominant positive view first, then reveals the disguised problem or hidden cost. No generic/boilerplate LinkedIn openers.
- [ ] Delivers at least one non-trivial, leverage-oriented insight for technically curious SWEs, staff engineers, or engineering leaders.
- [ ] Tone is direct, no-BS, and pragmatic. Cynical/sarcastic when it adds diagnostic sharpness. Nothing that reads as whiny.
- [ ] Highlights incentive distortions, measurement problems, tradeoffs, and real human/org dynamics.
- [ ] Closing delivers authoritative insight or direct advice from a senior technical leader perspective.
- [ ] Formatting follows the Formatting section above (negative space deliberate, unicode very light, italic over bold).
- [ ] Avoids buzzword soup, generic "AI changes everything" claims, ranty negativity, and over-used LinkedIn formulas.
- [ ] When natural, points to resources for readers who want to go deeper.

**Insight quality rubric** (posts with substantive claims or advice, any length or format):

- [ ] Core idea(s) feel earned and specific rather than generic or obvious.
- [ ] Advice or observations include mechanisms, implications, or tradeoffs (not just "do X").
- [ ] No shallow listicle syndrome: points have enough substance to hold value if condensed.
- [ ] Balanced, constructive framing on complex topics (especially AI/engineering shifts).
