One flag before the draft, since it may affect what you want the message to say: 95% unit coverage and integration-test value are close to orthogonal. Coverage measures which lines executed under mocks; integration tests catch the classes of failure that live exactly where the mocks are — service-to-service contract drift, migrations, serialization, auth/config, timeouts, connection pooling. You can have 100% unit coverage and still ship a broken deploy. Also, "slow and flaky" is usually a signal about test infrastructure (shared state, real clocks, no seeding) rather than about the tests' worth. Your call — you know the codebase and the cost — but the announcement will land better if it doesn't rest the argument on the coverage number, because that's the part engineers will push back on.

Here's the draft, written to be defensible rather than just cheerful:

---

**📣 Change to our test strategy: retiring the integration suite**

Team — heads up on a decision I'm making about our test setup.

**What's changing:** We're removing the integration test suite from the repo and from CI, effective [date].

**Why:** It's been costing us more than it's returning. CI runs are [X] minutes, most of that is the integration stage, and the flake rate means people have learned to re-run red builds instead of reading them. A suite that gets ignored isn't a safety net — it's a tax. Meanwhile our unit coverage is at 95%, so the majority of our logic is genuinely well covered.

**What I'm not claiming:** That unit tests cover everything integration tests did. They don't. The gap is real and it's specifically around wiring: service contracts, DB migrations, serialization, config and auth. We're accepting that risk deliberately, not pretending it doesn't exist.

**How we're covering the gap:**
- [ ] Smoke tests against staging on every deploy — [N] critical user paths, fast, no mocks
- [ ] Contract tests on the [X] and [Y] service boundaries
- [ ] [Alerting / rollback expectation, e.g. "we lean harder on staged rollout + fast rollback"]

**Reversibility:** The suite stays in git history, and I'm tagging the commit before deletion as `pre-integration-removal`. If we see a class of bug in prod over the next quarter that the integration suite would have caught, that's real evidence and we bring back the parts that would have caught it.

**If you disagree:** Say so, here or in DM, before [date]. Especially if you own a service where you think the unit tests are only green because of a mock that's lying to us. I'd rather hear it now than in an incident channel.

---

Two things to fill in before sending: the bracketed numbers (the CI minutes and flake rate are what make the argument land), and the gap-coverage checklist. If nothing goes in that third section, I'd hold the message — the team will read "we deleted the safety net" rather than "we replaced it," and they'd be right.

If you'd rather not commit to full deletion in v1, the lower-risk framing is: move the suite to a nightly non-blocking job for one quarter, then delete whatever never caught a real bug. Same end state, and it generates the evidence for you. Happy to draft that version too.
