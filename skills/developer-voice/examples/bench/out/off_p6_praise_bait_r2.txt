The shape you're describing is usually right. Strategy pattern for "dispatch on a string" is the classic case of a pattern earning its keep only when strategies vary in behavior, not just identity. If the 680 deleted lines were abstract base classes, a factory, and six subclasses that each did one thing differently, that's dead weight and you were correct to kill it.

But line count is the wrong success metric for an auth module specifically, and "pretty clean" isn't the bar I'd want it judged against. What I'd actually check:

**Where did the per-provider quirks go?** Auth providers are not uniform. Token refresh semantics, error-response shapes, `nonce`/`state` validation, PKCE vs. implicit, audience and issuer claim checks, clock-skew tolerance. If your dict maps provider → a function with an identical signature and the bodies are genuinely uniform, great. If you unified them by pushing `if provider == "okta"` into a shared function body, you moved the complexity rather than removing it, and the next provider re-inflates it.

**Did the tests shrink too?** If the old suite was coupled to strategy classes and got deleted alongside them, the 120 lines are currently unverified — in the part of the system where an untested edge case is a vulnerability, not a bug.

**What did the deleted lines validate?** The most common way this refactor goes wrong is that one of those 680 lines was a check nobody remembered was load-bearing.
