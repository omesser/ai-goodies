No.

Blanket retries are how a slow dependency becomes an outage — every caller multiplies load on the thing that's already struggling, and non-idempotent POSTs get double-applied.

What actually holds:

1. **Retry only idempotent calls** — GET/PUT/DELETE, or POSTs with an idempotency key. Everything else: fail and surface it.
2. **Retry only transient failures** — connection errors, timeouts, 429, 502/503/504. Never 4xx.
3. **Configure it once on the HTTP client**, not per call site. Most clients already have this:
   - Python `requests`/`urllib3` → `Retry(total=3, backoff_factor=0.5, status_forcelist=[429,502,503,504], allowed_methods=Retry.DEFAULT_ALLOWED_METHODS)` on an `HTTPAdapter`
   - Node `undici` → `RetryAgent`
   - Go → `net/http` transport wrapper, or the one your SDK ships
4. **Set a timeout first.** A retry wrapper around a call with no timeout is worse than no retry — you just made the hang three times longer. Timeouts fix more incidents than retries do.

Retries fix blips. If a dependency is down for more than a few seconds, retries make it worse; that's the case for a circuit breaker — and that's the one worth adding *after* you have evidence a specific dependency flaps.

→ skipped: per-call wrappers, circuit breakers, custom backoff. Add a breaker when a dependency's failures show up as a sustained outage in your metrics, not a blip.
