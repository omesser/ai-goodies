One flag before the draft: 95% unit coverage and integration coverage measure different things — unit tests with mocked collaborators can't catch wiring, serialization, schema drift, or real DB/network behavior, so "redundant at this coverage level" is the one claim in this announcement someone will push back on. The slow-and-flaky argument stands on its own and is harder to argue with. The draft leads with that instead, so it holds up in the thread.

**Draft:**

> **Retiring the integration test suite**
>
> Starting next sprint, we're removing the integration test suite from CI and the repo.
>
> Why: it's the slowest thing in our pipeline and the least trustworthy signal in it. Reruns on unrelated failures have become routine, and a test suite people rerun until it's green isn't catching anything. Meanwhile unit coverage is at 95% this quarter, so the bulk of our logic is verified in a form that runs fast and fails honestly.
>
> What this means day to day:
> - CI gets meaningfully faster.
> - No more "just hit retry" on red builds.
> - Unit tests are now the gate. If you're adding behavior, the unit test is not optional.
>
> What I want to hear about: if there's a failure mode you believe *only* the integration suite catches — a wiring bug, a migration, a contract with an external service — reply in this thread by [date]. I'd rather keep a small number of targeted tests than discover the gap in production.
>
> Happy to talk through the reasoning with anyone who wants the longer version.

Two things worth considering before you send:

- **The invitation to object is doing real work.** It converts a decision people might resent into one they had input on, and it surfaces the specific gaps cheaply. If you'd rather not open the floor, cut that paragraph — but then expect the objections in DMs instead.
- **Consider keeping ~5 smoke tests** covering signup, checkout, or whatever your critical paths are. It costs almost nothing in CI time, kills the "we have no end-to-end safety net" objection outright, and makes the announcement land as a cleanup rather than a removal.
