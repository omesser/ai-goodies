One flag before the draft: 95% unit coverage and integration coverage aren't the same axis. Line coverage tells you which lines ran under mocks; integration tests catch the class of failure unit tests structurally can't see — broken wiring between components, schema/contract drift, config and migration errors, auth, real serialization, third-party client behavior. And "flaky" is often a real race condition reporting itself rather than test-harness noise. So the redundancy claim is the part of this I'd expect the team to push back on hardest.

That's your call to make, and the draft below makes the case as strongly as it can be made. I've written it to invite the pushback rather than pre-empt it, because that pushback is cheaper now than after a wiring bug ships.

```
:wrench: Change to our test strategy: retiring the integration suite

We crossed 95% unit test coverage this quarter — big milestone, and real credit to
everyone who chipped away at it. On the back of that, I've decided to retire our
integration test suite.

The reasoning:
• Runtime — the integration suite dominates our CI time and is the main thing standing
  between a push and a green build.
• Flakiness — intermittent failures have trained us to re-run rather than investigate,
  which makes the signal close to worthless.
• Overlap — at 95% unit coverage, the large majority of what the integration suite
  exercises is already covered at a faster, more reliable layer.

What this means day to day:
• CI should get substantially faster, and green should start meaning green again.
• Unit tests become our primary safety net. That raises the bar on them: if you're
  touching a boundary — API contracts, DB access, config, auth, serialization — make
  sure there's a unit test that actually pins the behavior, not just one that
  executes the line.
• The suite is being removed from CI, not erased from history. It stays in git, so
  anything we discover we miss can be pulled back.

Where I want your input:
If there's a specific integration test you believe is load-bearing — something it
catches that no unit test can — flag it in this thread by <date>. I'd rather keep a
handful of high-value tests than discover the gap in production. I'm genuinely open to
being wrong on specific cases here.

Happy to talk through the decision in more detail — grab me or drop questions below.
```

Two things I'd change if you want a materially lower-risk version:

- **Swap "retiring" for "quarantining."** Move the suite to a nightly, non-blocking job instead of deleting it. You get the entire CI speed win — the suite stops blocking pushes — while keeping the signal. This removes the whole redundancy argument from the message, which is the weakest part of it.
- **Set a review date.** Add "we'll revisit in 90 days and look at whether escaped-defect rate moved." It converts an irreversible decision into a testable one, and it's the single easiest thing to say if a wiring bug does ship.
